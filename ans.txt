Clear(x)	No block is placed on x
Holding(x)	Robot is holding block x
HandEmpty	Robot's hand is empty

The standard STRIPS actions are:

Action 1: PICKUP(x)

Preconditions:
- OnTable(x)
- Clear(x)
- HandEmpty
Effects:
- Add: Holding(x)
- Delete: OnTable(x), Clear(x), HandEmpty
Action 2: PUTDOWN(x)

Precondition:

- Holding(x)

Effects:
- Add: OnTable(x), Clear(x), HandEmpty
- Delete: Holding(x)
Action 3: STACK(x,y)

Preconditions:
- Holding(x)
- Clear(y)
Effects:
- Add: On(x,y), Clear(x), HandEmpty

Action 4: UNSTACK(x,y)

Preconditions:
- On(x,y)
- Clear(x)
- HandEmpty
Effects:
- Add: Holding(x), Clear(y)
- Delete: On(x,y), Clear(x), HandEmpty

Q2. Knowledge-Based System – First-Order Logic
(i) Represent all four university policies using FOL
We need to translate each university policy into First-Order Logic.

Policy 1: Every student studies at least one course.
\[
\boxed{
\forall x(Student(x)\rightarrow
\exists y(Course(y)\land Studies(x,y)))
}
\]

Explanation: For every student x, there exists at least one course y such that x studies y.

Policy 2: A student who studies and passes the same course is qualified for research.
\[
\boxed{
\forall x\forall y[
(Student(x)\land Course(y)\land
Studies(x,y)\land Passed(x,y))
\rightarrow Qualified(x)
]
}
\]

Explanation: If a student studies a course and passes that same course, the student becomes qualified for research.

Policy 3: A qualified student supervised by a professor is a researcher.
\[
\boxed{
\forall x\forall y[
(Qualified(x)\land Professor(y)\land
Supervises(y,x))
\rightarrow Researcher(x)
]
}
\]

Explanation: If a student is qualified and a professor supervises that student, the student is considered a researcher.

Policy 4: Every researcher is eligible for a research fellowship.
\[
\boxed{
\forall x(Researcher(x)\rightarrow Eligible(x))
}
\]

Explanation: Every researcher is eligible for the fellowship.

(ii) Does the given statement imply that every student studies the same course? Can we conclude that every student studies AI?
Given:
\[
\forall x(Student(x)\rightarrow
\exists y(Course(y)\land Studies(x,y)))
\]

Answer 1: Does every student study the same course?

No.

The existential quantifier \(\exists y\) is inside the universal quantifier \(\forall x\).

This means that for every student, there exists at least one course that the particular student studies. The course can be different for different students.

For example:
- Ravi studies AI.
- Anu studies ML.
Both satisfy the given statement, even though they study different courses.

Answer 2: Can we conclude that every student studies AI?

No.

The statement only guarantees that every student studies at least one course. It does not specify which course.

From the given knowledge base:
\[
Studies(Ravi,AI)
\]

\[
Studies(Anu,ML)
\]

Therefore, we cannot conclude:
\[
\boxed{\forall x(Student(x)\rightarrow Studies(x,AI))}
\]

Final conclusion:
1. Every student studies at least one course.
2. Different students can study different courses.
3. We cannot conclude that every student studies AI.
